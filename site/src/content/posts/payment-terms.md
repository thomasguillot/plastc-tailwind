Most invoices are read once, quickly, by someone who did not hire you. They look for three things: how much, by when, and how to pay. If any of those takes more than a few seconds to find, the invoice goes to the bottom of the pile.

## Put the due date next to the total

“Net 30” on its own asks the reader to count days from an issue date they may not remember. Write the date: **Due Dec 24**. Keep it directly beside the amount, in the same size, so the two are read together. Plastc does this by default, and shows the date again on every reminder.

## Name the late fee plainly

“Late fee” is clear. “Finance charge”, “penalty interest” and “administrative surcharge” are not. Say what it is, what it costs and when it starts: *A late fee of 2% applies to any balance unpaid after the due date.* One sentence, in the terms line, not in the footer.

## When net 30 is the wrong choice

- Small jobs under a few hundred dollars. Ask for payment on receipt, or net 7. A month is a long time to wait for an amount that is easy to pay today.
- Customers who pay in weekly or biweekly runs. Match their cycle, or you will land just after a run and wait nearly two weeks longer than you planned.
- Large projects. Split them into a deposit and a final invoice, each with its own date, instead of one big invoice with one long term.

## Keep one set of terms

Customers remember what they were told last. If your quote says net 14, your contract says net 30 and your invoice says net 21, expect the longest. Pick one, set it as the account default, and change it per customer only when you have agreed to it in writing.
