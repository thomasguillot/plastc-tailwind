Chasing an invoice is awkward because it feels like an accusation. It rarely is. Most late invoices are late because they were lost, not because anyone decided not to pay. A calm, specific message each time gets you paid sooner than a firm one.

## Day one: a note

Send it the morning after the due date. Assume the invoice slipped past someone.

> Hi Dana, a quick note that invoice INV-0191 for $9,300 was due yesterday. The pay link is below if it is easier. If it is already on its way, thank you, and please ignore this.

## Day seven: a reminder

By now it is worth asking whether something is wrong. Offer an answer to the most likely problems.

> Hi Dana, INV-0191 is now a week overdue. If you need a purchase order number on it, or it should go to a different address, tell me and I will fix it today. Otherwise the pay link is here.

## Day thirty: a call

Stop writing. Call, and ask when you can expect payment. Then write down the date they give you and send it back to them in a short email, so there is a record you both agreed to.

## A few rules

- Always include the invoice number, the amount and the pay link. Nobody should have to search for them.
- Use the customer’s first name and your own. A message from a person is answered faster than one from a system.
- If they reply, stop the schedule. Plastc pauses reminders for you when a customer writes back.
