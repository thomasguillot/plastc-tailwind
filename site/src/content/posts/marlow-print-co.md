Marlow Print Co. makes packaging and printed menus for eleven wholesale customers, most of them restaurants and small retailers. Orders ship every week, so invoices go out every week. Until this summer, the person who sent them was also the person who ran the press.

## What Sunday evenings looked like

Naveen Marlow did the books on Sunday evening, after the shop had closed. He exported the week’s orders, typed each one into a spreadsheet, and sent the invoices from his own email. Chasing the unpaid ones came after that, one message at a time, from a list he kept in a notes app.

“I knew who was late,” he said. “I just did not want to be the person who wrote to them every week, so I waited, and then it was a month.”

## What changed

Marlow moved to weekly invoices created from a template for each customer, with reminders switched on at day one, day seven and day fourteen after the due date. The reminders come from his shop’s name and carry a pay link, so a customer can settle an invoice from the email in under a minute.

He kept one rule for himself: anything over 30 days gets a phone call, never another email.

## The result

Invoices sent per month went from 18 in June to 29 in October as he added customers, and the Sunday session dropped from about three hours to twenty minutes. The share of invoices paid within a week of the due date went from roughly half to most of them. He still calls the slowest payer, but now it is one customer, not four.

“The press is the part of the job I like,” he said. “Now the books stay out of my weekend.”
