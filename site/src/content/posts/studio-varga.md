Studio Varga is two architects, Mila and Ondrej Varga, with fifteen active clients between them. They design houses, extensions and a few small public buildings, and they are paid in stages. For years, billing happened whenever one of them remembered.

## One day a week

Last January they picked Friday. Every invoice goes out that day, whether it is for a deposit, a milestone or a monthly retainer. If a stage finishes on Tuesday, the invoice waits until Friday.

“It sounded like we were losing three days,” Mila said. “In practice, clients learned to expect it. Payment dates became predictable, and so did ours.”

## What they send

Each client has a stage plan in Plastc, so the Friday session is mostly checking that the right amounts are ready and pressing send. A retainer client gets the same invoice on the same Friday every month. Everyone else gets one when a stage closes.

Reminders go out on the Tuesday after the due date, which keeps them away from the weekend and from the day the invoice arrived.

## What they would tell another small practice

- Pick the day first, then the tool. The habit does more than the software.
- Agree payment terms in the proposal, not on the invoice.
- Keep the invoice short. One line per stage is enough.

Invoices sent on Fridays went from four in January to nine in October, as the practice took on more projects, and Ondrej says he has not thought about billing on a Monday since.
